# GERUNDS AND INFINITIVES

B2 Cheat Sheet

## 1 INFINITIVES

### 1. To express purpose

- I’m studying hard **to pass** my exams.
- She saved some money **to buy** a new laptop.
- They left early **to avoid** the traffic.
- He called his lawyer **to ask** for advice.
- We went to Madrid **to visit** the Prado Museum.
- I turned down the music **to concentrate**.
- We installed a security system **to protect** our home.

### 2. Adjective + infinitive

- I’m glad **to see** you.
- It’s important **to plan** ahead.
- She was lucky **to win** the prize.
- He’s easy **to talk to**.
- It was kind of you **to help** me.
- We’re disappointed **to hear** the news.
- It’s essential **to drink** enough water.
- I was surprised **to find** you here.
- They were excited **to travel** abroad.
- He’s too tired **to go** out tonight.

### 3. Some verbs + infinitive (no change in meaning)

- **want** — I want **to** improve my English.
- **need** — You need **to** rest more.
- **would like** — I’d like **to speak** to you.
- **forget** — Don’t forget **to** lock the door.
- **decide** — I decided **to** accept the offer.
- **pretend** (fingir) — He pretended **to** be interested.
- **remember** — Remember to email me.
- **promise** — I promise **to** call you later.
- **refuse** — She refused **to** answer.
- **expect + object** — I expect you to arrive on time.
- **seem** (parece) — He seems **to** be happy.
- **arrange** — I arranged **to** meet her on Friday.

## 2 GERUNDS -ING

### 1. Verb as a subject

- **Travelling** broadens the mind.
- **Reading** widely improves your vocabulary.
- **Swimming** every morning keeps you fit.
- **Learning** from mistakes is essential.
- **Patience** is a virtue.
- **Time management** is crucial in today’s world.
- **Being** honest builds trust.
- **Overthinking** can lead to stress.
- **Taking** risks is sometimes necessary.
- **Making** good decisions is not always easy.

### 2. Preposition + gerund

- I’m interested **in learning** Spanish.
- She’s skilled **at solving** problems.
- We’re **used to waking** up early.
- He’s tired **of hearing** the same excuses.
- I’m looking **forward to seeing** you.
- I’m terrified **of flying**.
- I’m proud **of what** you’ve achieved.
- He’s responsible **for managing** the team.
- They’re accused **of cheating**.
- I’m aware **of the risks** involved.
- We’re capable **of achieving** more.
- She insisted **on paying** the bill.

### 3. Some verbs + gerund

- **enjoy** — I enjoy **playing** the piano.
- **don’t mind** (no importa) — I don’t mind **waiting**.
- **keep + gerund = continue** — He kept **talking** during the class.
- **feel like** (apetecer) — I feel like **going** for a run.
- **can’t stand** (no soportar) — I can’t stand **listening** to that song.
- **look forward to** — We’re looking forward to **hearing** from you.
- **have trouble** — She has trouble concentrating.
- **spend time / waste time** — He spends too much time on social media.
- **be worth** — It’s worth trying.
- **finish** — I finished **reading** the book.
- **practise** — She practises yoga every day.

## 3 VERBS THAT TAKE BOTH

### Same meaning

**begin** — It began to rain. = It began raining.

**start** — He started to study. = He started studying.

**continue** — She continued to work. = She continued working.

**try** — I tried to fix it. = I tried fixing it.

**prefer** — I prefer to read. = I prefer reading.

**like** — I like to cook. = I like cooking.

**hate** — I hate to wait. = I hate waiting.

**love** — I love to travel. = I love travelling.

**plan** — I plan to visit Rome. = I plan visiting Rome.

**intend** — I intend to apply. = I intend applying.

### Different meaning

**Stop**

1. I stopped to **help** the old man. (I stopped in order to help him.)
2. I stopped **smoking**. (I quit smoking.)
3. He stopped to **think**. (He paused in order to think.)
4. He stopped **thinking** about the problem. (He no longer thinks about it.)

**Remember**

1. Remember to **send** the email. (Don’t forget to send it.)
2. I remember **sending** the email yesterday. (I have a memory of **doing** it.)
3. Remember to **bring** your ID. (Don’t forget.)
4. I remember **meeting** him at the conference. (I have a memory of it.)

## 4 NEGATIVE FORMS

> **When the infinitive or gerund is negative, use ‘not’ before the infinitive or gerund.**

- She promised **not to** insult me again! But she did it anyway!
- I don’t like **not knowing** the answer. — He avoided answering the question.
- I’m looking forward **not to having** any meetings tomorrow.
- He refused **not to** apologise.
- I regret **not applying** for the scholarship.
- They remembered **not to forget** their passports.
